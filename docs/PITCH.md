# Rush: pitch

**Hook:** a dinner service played like XCOM. The tickets are the enemy.

**Status:** one-week side test next to Lethemoor, started Nov 24, 2026. Lethemoor stays the active
game. Full plan with proof and market check: the "Games People Want: Nov 2026 Scan" doc.

## Three pillars (every feature must serve one, or it's cut)
1. **The rush is the enemy.** Tickets come in waves with timers; the rail shows the next 3 coming.
2. **Every move is a call.** Turn-based, no reflexes. Cooks have 3 actions a turn; one turn is one
   minute of service.
3. **A real kitchen.** 86, all day, heard, refires, the 12-top at 7:40. Built by a line cook.

## Core loop
- **30 seconds:** read the rail, move a cook, FIRE / PLATE / RUN, END TURN.
- **5 minutes:** survive a push: a big table lands, a pan burns, a cook is close to walking out.
- **A session (~10 min):** one full service, graded on tickets out, tips and tables walked.

## Comparables
- Into the Breach: same readable "what's coming" rail, but the threat is tickets, not mechs.
- PlateUp / Overcooked: real-time co-op chaos; Rush is solo and turn-based, one thumb on a phone.
- XCOM: squad stakes; cooks walk out under stress instead of dying.

## Platform and price
Android APK first (dev release + in-app updates), then a free web demo (itch, CrazyGames).
Premium ~$4.99 to $6.99 with the first service free. No ads, no energy timers.

## Not in this game
- Online multiplayer or co-op
- A real-time mode
- 3D
- Decorating or tycoon building
- More than one restaurant in v1
- A story campaign with cutscenes

## Kill criteria (any one parks Rush; #5 "the game where you get weaker" moves up)
- The side test ends (Dec 1, 2026) without a slice Duff wants to keep playing.
- Of 5 testers, fewer than 2 start a second service without being asked.
- Fun under 6/10 after two rounds of fixes.
- Turns feel like busywork: testers say it's slow or end turns without reading the rail.
