# Rush (build 89) - IGN-style review

**Score: 7.0 / 10** - A clean, readable turn-based kitchen tactics game with real planning in it, held back by fiddly touch targets, easy early services, and a presentation that's competent rather than distinctive.

## What I played
Chromium, 390x780 touch, about 180 taps. I skipped the optional HOW TO PLAY and learned from the in-game coach hints.
- **Service 1: A.** "7 of 7 out, 0 walks, Tips $51", "88 POINTS: out 60, speed 13, pans 15". Two cooks (DEV, MO), grill and fry only.
- Shop after service 1: bought SERVER TRAY ($30; the screen read "register: server tray bought"), $21 left. I couldn't afford anything else.
- **Service 2: A.** "8 of 8 out, 0 walks, Tips $57", 88 points again. SAUTE opened ("NEW STATION: SAUTE ... Now on the menu: pasta and scampi."). A 12-top (T7, BIG) arrived. Shop: bought TEMP COOK ($45) and HEAT LAMPS ($30), $3 left.
- **Service 3: in progress, not finished.** COLD opened ("NEW STATION: COLD ... salad and cake."). Tonight's twist was "HEAT WAVE: The line is an oven: working cooks take +1 stress every other turn." JO, the temp cook, joined as a third cook. At my last screenshot (7:05 PM, OUT 1) COLD had burned ("BURN ON COLD, REFIRE | 12-TOP ON THE RAIL"), "6 TO FIRE" was stacked on COLD, and a +2 MORE badge hid more tickets. It was heading toward a loss or a poor grade.
- **Losses: none.** Services cleared: 2 of 5. I didn't reach 4 or 5, mainly because of the tap budget. A lot of taps went on mis-taps, covered below.

## Gameplay depth
The core idea works well. Tickets are the enemy, a clock ticks in minutes, and each cook has about 3 action points per turn. You FIRE a dish at a station, it cooks for N minutes, you PLATE it before it burns, then a cook RUNS it from the pass. Position matters. Stations show "WALK 1/2/3" or "TOO FAR", walking spends the same points as cooking, and a cook can't do everything.

The best decisions are small and tactical:
- Which table is URGENT, and which dish do you fire first? You can tap a ticket to prioritise.
- Do you RUSH ("+1 STRESS") to save a minute, or hold it?
- Who stands at the pass, and when do you send someone to a station that is "TOO FAR"?
- The "STRESS 1/6 -1" meters and the "FULL STRESS = QUITS" rule add a real second resource.

Between services the shop adds a light meta layer with six upgrades: FAMILY MEAL, HEAT LAMPS, SERVER TRAY/ENTREE TRAY, TEMP COOK, CAST IRON, SECOND CHANCE. The text "Every 2 upgrades bring +1 table" is a good rubber-band mechanic, because buying power has a visible cost.

Where depth is thin:
- Services 1 and 2 are mostly "fire, wait, plate, run" with little pressure.
- Dish variety is just a station name plus a cook time. There are no recipes with multiple steps and no ingredients.
- Every cook is functionally identical (DEV, MO and JO have no skills or perks), so cook choice is only about position.

## Difficulty curve
- Service 1 is a tutorial. I got an A with spare time and no near-misses.
- Service 2 added SAUTE and a BIG table and still gave an A with zero walks. It was comfortable.
- Service 3 is where the game turns on. A new station, a third cook who starts out of position, HEAT WAVE and a 12-top arrive at once. COLD burned within about three turns, because I couldn't both serve the pass and tend three stations.
- This is a believable difficulty spike for a roguelike-ish loop. But 1 to 2 is flat and 2 to 3 is steep. I got the same 88 points on both clears, so the grade bar doesn't make you push for A+.
- I didn't get to services 4 or 5.

## Clarity and UI
Strong points:
- The ticket rail's colour-coded timers and plain-language rows ("at GRILL", "cooking", "on the pass", "served" struck through) read quickly.
- Station badges ("1 TO FIRE", "PLATE NOW", "TOO FAR: MO NEEDS 3 MOVES, HAS 2") say what is wrong.
- The button labels adapt: "DEV: FIRE T30", "MO: RUN 2 PLATES", "END TURN / GRILL BURNS", "END TURN / FINISH SERVICE". It is rare for a mobile game to explain consequences this directly.
- Coach hints ("FULL STRESS = QUITS", "DONE = NO MOVES", "TAP TICKET: COOK FIRST") arrive just in time.

Weak points:
- **Tap precision is the main problem.** Stations are narrow (about 75 px wide), cook portraits overlap, and the cooks move around, so a tap that worked last turn hits a different target next turn. Several times a tap on the pass or a station did nothing or selected the wrong cook, and I lost actions. I had to re-screenshot and re-tap more than once.
- The two-tap model (pick a cook, then a station) is not obvious at first. The coach hint "TAP FACE, THEN STATION" appears late.
- Busy states overflow the screen. The ticket rail hides tickets behind "+2 MORE" and the board gets crowded.
- Dots and numbers on the cook badge (moves left) are small.
- Dishes and walk costs vary between services, and the cost of an action is not always visible before you tap it.

## Presentation
- **Art:** A painted kitchen backdrop with hanging pans and a hood, and chunky station cards with cartoony food icons. It is cohesive and warm but static. Cooks are portrait badges, not characters in the kitchen, so the "walking" is just a portrait sliding along the rail.
- **Sound:** I couldn't judge audio in the headless run. On-screen captions such as "[printer: new ticket]" and "[register: server tray bought]" suggest an intentional sound layer.
- **Feel:** The stamped grade, the new-station modals ("HEARD!") and the twist cards are nice moments. Feedback on each action is thin: burgers don't sizzle visually and plating doesn't feel crunchy. Overall it feels like a polished prototype, not a juicy game.

## Replayability
- Each service gets harder, with new stations and twists (HEAT WAVE, 12-top). The shop gives some build variety, and the service count and temp cook choice make runs differ.
- Weaknesses: the same two cooks, the same station set, no persistent unlocks I could see, and difficulty that is mostly "more tickets". After 4 or 5 services I'd expect the loop to feel familiar. A daily seed, a chef perk, or menu variation would extend it.
- A grade system (A+/A/B...) gives chase goals, but the score floor (88 points on easy services) isn't pushing me.

## Pros
- Genuine tactical decisions: action points, walking costs, stress, rush, trays.
- Excellent contextual text on buttons and badges that says exactly what will happen.
- The ticket rail and station state are readable at a glance in calm moments.
- Good pacing of new content: a new station and a twist per service.
- Light, meaningful shop with a rubber-band on tickets.
- Short sessions, works on a phone in portrait, and loads quickly.

## Cons
- Touch targets are small and shift; mis-taps cost real actions.
- Services 1 and 2 are too easy, then service 3 jumps sharply.
- Cooks are identical; no recipes, no cook skills.
- The busy board gets cramped; tickets hide behind "+N MORE".
- Visual feedback and animation are limited; the kitchen is a backdrop, not a living place.
- I found the HOW TO PLAY and first-turn flow only adequate: new players must infer the face-then-station model.

## Top 3 changes, ranked
1. **Make the touch input forgiving.** Enlarge station hit areas (the full column, including the cook portrait region), stop cooks from overlapping the stations' tap zones, and add an undo or confirm for a wasted move. Add "tap station with a selected cook = do the obvious action".
2. **Smooth and sharpen the curve.** Make service 2 demand a real choice (e.g. introduce the BIG table or a twist earlier), and soften the service 3 cliff (don't stack a new station, a third out-of-position cook and a twist at once). Make the grade formula harder to max so an A is earned.
3. **Give cooks and dishes identity.** Add per-cook perks (a fast grill cook, a stress-resistant cook), multi-step or longer dishes, and visible animation of cooks and food. That would turn a good puzzle into a game with character and long-term replay.

## Verdict
Rush has a sound, readable tactical core and excellent text feedback, but it's under-juiced and its input is fiddly. I'd score it **7/10**: good for a free browser game, and not yet a standout.
